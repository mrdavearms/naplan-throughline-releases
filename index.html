Opening it the first time — the security warning is normal

Because the app is free and not paid Apple/Microsoft code-signed, the first time you open it your computer asks you to confirm. This is expected and does not mean anything is wrong — you only do it once, then it opens like any other app. (If your browser warns the download is “not commonly downloaded” or tries to discard it, choose Keep.)

On a Mac

  1. Open the downloaded .dmg and drag NAPLAN Cohort Tracker into your Applications folder.
  2. Open your Applications folder and double-click the app. macOS will say it “could not verify” the app — that's expected. Click Done (do not click Move to Trash).
  3. Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down to the Security section, and next to “NAPLAN Cohort Tracker was blocked” click Open Anyway. Confirm with your fingerprint or Mac password.
  4. On older Macs you can instead skip steps 2–3 and just right-click (or Control-click) the app → Open → Open.

After this first time, open it normally from Applications, the Dock or Launchpad.

On Windows

  1. Run the downloaded setup .exe.
  2. If you see a blue “Windows protected your PC” box, click More info → Run anyway.

Early release  May not work properly

This is an early release and is still being tested, so some figures or screens may not work as expected — please check anything important against your source spreadsheets before you rely on it. The Year 3 → Year 5 (primary) and new Year 5 → Year 7 (P–12) cohort tracking are the newest parts and the least validated, so treat those figures as especially provisional.

Your feedback is genuinely welcome — tell me what works, what doesn't, and anything that looks off: dave.armstrong@education.vic.gov.au.

What it does

Point it at a folder of your NAPLAN SSSR files and it shows participation, proficiency, equity and skill gaps — and tracks the same students across two years (Year 3 to Year 5, Year 7 to Year 9, or Year 5 to Year 7 in a combined P–12 school), the closest thing to a true measure of your school's contribution. It builds tidy PDF reports for your leadership and faculty conversations.

Private by design  Everything runs on your computer. No student names appear anywhere, and no data is ever uploaded.

What's new in 1.2.0

Clearer cohort tracking (Section 10) — same private, on-device tool, easier to read and act on:

  • Student counts beside every percentage — "−8.5 pp · 6 fewer students at Needs additional support", not just a percentage that can look dramatic on a small group.
  • An honest "is this meaningful?" note — tells you when your cohort is too small for a change to be statistically significant, instead of implying false certainty.
  • A "students who improved" list to recognise gains, alongside the follow-up list.
  • Follow-up across domains — the students who slipped or stalled in more than one subject, gathered as your clearest intervention priority.
  • Clearer leavers & joiners — who left, who joined, and how that shapes the headline figure.
